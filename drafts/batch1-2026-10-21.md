## How the timing math works

- **Speech estimate:** words × 60 ÷ rate. Rates are 90 wpm for English and 70 wpm for Spanish, as in the earlier draft. These are estimates from word counts, not measurements.
- **Spanish closing:** the line is 3.6 s, taken from the measured `es-arrive-014` (3.605 s in `files-2b.tsv`).
- **Silence:** exact durations, since they are generated clips.
- **Total:** speech + silence = 180 s ±1 s for every script and language. Every table below sums to this.
- **Silence structure:** one lead-in clip (music alone), one silence clip after every narration line except the last, and one tail clip after the closing (music alone). Spanish speech runs longer than English, so its silence clips are shorter.
- **Accuracy:** treat the totals as ±15% until measured clip durations replace the estimates. ElevenLabs clips may carry leading and trailing silence that `report-2b.md` says was not checked, so measure with ffprobe before the mix.

**Clip naming:**
- **Narration:** `<lang>-<slug>-NNN_<settings tag>.mp3`, numbered from 000 in line order. This follows `incoming/`, for example `en-arrive-000_sp72_s60_sb75_se0_m2.mp3`.
  - English tag: `_sp102_s60_sb75_se0_m2` (provisional)
  - Spanish tag: `_sp82_s60_sb75_se0_m2`
  - Slugs: `en-open-the-day`, `es-abre-el-dia`, `en-clarity`, `es-claridad`, `en-before-sleep`, `es-antes-de-dormir`
- **Silence:** `sil-<lang>-<script slug>-NNN_<seconds>s.wav`, numbered from 000 in mix order. These are proposed to be made locally (for example with ffmpeg), not by ElevenLabs. They cost no credits and carry no ElevenLabs settings tag.
- **Spanish closing option:** `es-arrive-014_sp82_s60_sb75_se0_m2.mp3` already holds the exact Spanish closing and could replace the three new closing clips. I planned new clips for consistency and flag the reuse as an option.
